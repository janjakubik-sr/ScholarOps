# AI-Generated Text Detection: Evidence and Practice Knowledge Base

## Administrative Metadata

- **Resource ID:** `AI-Detector-KB`
- **Version:** `1.0.0`
- **Last targeted literature verification (UTC):** `2026-11-23`
- **Status:** Construction-time, bounded baseline review (`ONLINE_PARTIAL` for exhaustive current-literature purposes). Unscreened discovery results remain possible; this is not a claim that no newer work exists. This date never substitutes for the live verification required before every analysis.
- **Companion agent:** `../AI-Detector.md`
- **Scope:** Passage-level assessment of possible LLM involvement in natural-language text. The literature is disproportionately English-language and benchmark-based; transfer to other languages, genres, short spans, edited text, and unseen generators is uncertain.
- **Excluded uses:** Determining intent or misconduct, identifying a natural person, assigning sensitive traits, or making a high-stakes decision without independent evidence and human review.

## 1. Operative Conclusions

1. **AI-generated text detection is conditional, not a universal property of prose.** Results depend on the generator, decoding, prompt, domain, language, text length, editing, calibration data, detector, and adversarial setting [K01–K02, K10–K16, K20].
2. **Text-only review supports suspicion, not definitive detection.** Fluency, predictability, uniform rhythm, generic phrasing, repeated structure, or isolated vocabulary are not uniquely machine-generated. Text-internal stylistic support is capped at confidence **1 (low)** [K01–K04, K08–K11, K19].
3. **No document-wide “AI percentage.”** Do not average, count, or interpolate passage scores into a proportion of AI authorship. Even research systems explicitly designed for numeric co-authorship ratios describe fine-grained attribution as far from solved [K15].
4. **A detector score is not P(AI-generated | text).** AUROC, accuracy, confidence, perplexity, and vendor percentages do not become posterior probabilities without a validated sampling frame, base rate, and calibration for the same setting [K01–K02, K10–K11, K17–K18].
5. **Fine-grained claims need finer justification.** Sentence and word boundaries are difficult to infer, short spans provide less evidence, and mixed human–AI writing invalidates an assumption that a document has one uniform origin [K04, K14–K16].
6. **External provenance outranks stylistic inference.** Verify generation records, edit history, content credentials, or a source corpus where possible. Even verified AI origin does not establish deception or who pasted the text [K07, K12–K13].
7. **False positives and subgroup/style bias are first-order concerns.** Evaluate human explanations and relevant human controls; never infer language background or identity. Professionally edited human writing can move detector scores in opposite directions across tools, so neither polished nor formulaic prose is an authorship marker [K08–K09, K18, K20–K23].

## 2. Evidence Hierarchy

Use the strongest evidence actually available; do not inflate weaker evidence by listing it repeatedly.

| Tier | Evidence | Permissible use | Main restrictions | Maximum confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Directly verifiable generation or provenance record tied to the span: authenticated provider history/export, trusted version history showing generation, signed content credentials with a validated chain, or an exact/approximate match to a separately authenticated AI-generated source | Can support **Detected — AI-origin externally established** for the matched passage | Validate identity, time, span correspondence, and chain of custody. Distinguish quotation/copying from composition. Do not infer intent or the user’s behaviour | **3** only when direct, specific, independently checkable, and material alternatives are resolved |
| B | Correctly applied, known-scheme watermark result with verified model/scheme configuration, sufficient span, documented null distribution, and validation under comparable editing | Independent computational corroboration; normally **Suspected** | Absence is not human authorship. Paraphrase, insertion, deletion, translation, mixed authorship, and spoofing matter. A raw watermark score alone is not definitive provenance [K07, K12–K13] | **2**; **3** only if independent provenance corroboration also satisfies Tier A |
| C | Reproducible computational detector or model-likelihood method actually run with recorded software/model versions, settings, threshold, passage length, and directly relevant validation/calibration | **Suspected**, with bounded support | Never compute a score mentally. Benchmark performance is conditional; unseen domains/models and attacks degrade results [K05–K06, K10–K13, K17–K18]. An unverified user-reported score does not count as reproduced Tier C evidence | **2** only when every confidence-2 condition in §4.2 is demonstrated; otherwise **1** |
| D | Systematic within-document comparison and matched writing samples from the same author, genre, language, task, and period | Exploratory **Suspected** or **Inconclusive** | Matched samples reduce but do not eliminate confounding. Editing, translation, and different assistance can yield the same surface text | **1** without valid Tier B/C corroboration |
| E | Text-internal observations: repeated rhetorical scaffolds, discourse uniformity, lexical/syntactic repetition, abrupt local style changes, context-insensitive genericity | Generate review hypotheses only | These are not diagnostic markers. Multiple correlated prose cues count as one weak evidence family, not independent confirmation [K01–K04, K19] | **1** |
| F | Isolated buzzwords, em dashes, polished grammar, “burstiness” estimated by eye, factual errors, fabricated references, personal impression, or a model asking itself whether it wrote the text | No AI-origin classification | Plausible human explanations are too numerous; factual/citation checks concern reliability, not provenance | **0** |

When tiers conflict, report the conflict. Strong external evidence can lower an AI-origin hypothesis as well as raise it; do not selectively retain only positive evidence.

## 3. Method Families and Their Limits

### 3.1 Provenance and source comparison

Prefer provider records, document histories, drafts, authenticated generation logs, and signed provenance over classification. Preserve identifiers and independently checkable links or hashes. A verified match establishes the origin of the matched text, not that its submitter intended deception. Retrieval against a provider-maintained generation corpus can defend against paraphrase but requires access to that corpus and controls false matches [K12].

### 3.2 Watermarking

Some generation schemes deliberately bias token selection and test the resulting statistical signal [K07]. A valid result is scheme-specific, not a generic property of LLM text. Record the generator, scheme/version, key or verification procedure where applicable, token/span length, test statistic and null model, threshold, transformations, and comparable validation. Paraphrasing can evade watermarks and learned signatures can be spoofed; neither a negative result nor a lone positive score proves authorship [K12–K13].

### 3.3 Supervised neural classifiers and commercial detectors

These learn human/generated distinctions from examples. Their performance depends on representativeness and thresholds; unseen models, domains, languages, short social texts, and attacks can cause substantial degradation [K01–K02, K08–K11, K20, K24]. Tool comparisons have found inaccuracy, inconsistency, false positives, false negatives, and vulnerability to obfuscation [K08, K23]. Paired human manuscripts and their professionally edited versions demonstrate that style changes alone can change scores inconsistently across detectors [K22]. Vendor labels and percentages are not independent external proof. Privacy permission is required before sending supplied text to any third-party service.

### 3.4 Model-likelihood and zero-shot statistics

Perplexity, entropy, rank, probability curvature, and two-model contrasts exploit different statistical regularities. DetectGPT requires suitable model log-probabilities and perturbations [K05]; Binoculars contrasts a pair of pretrained models [K06]. Neither method can be simulated by visual inspection. Their published benchmark numbers are not transferable probabilities for another passage. Length, source/proxy model mismatch, sampling, prompt, domain, and paraphrase affect results [K02, K10–K15].

### 3.5 Human and language-model review

Human and automatic systems can rely on different cues; longer passages can help both, and human judgments remain fallible [K04]. One 2025 study found that a majority vote of five frequent ChatGPT users performed exceptionally on its 300-article test and often cited vocabulary and sentence structure [K19]. That result does **not** validate one unaided reader, a language model’s self-assessment, other annotator populations, or passage-level decisions. Use lexical/rhetorical observations only as weak, contextual clues and retain alternative explanations. For decision support, make scores secondary to inspectable alternatives, limitations, and contestable reasoning. One controlled fixed-stimulus study supports its specific complete interface, not arbitrary generated rationales or other systems [K26].

### 3.6 Mixed-authorship and span-level attribution

Human drafting, AI drafting, AI editing, translation, human revision, and quotation can coexist. Document-level classifiers cannot simply be transferred to sentences or words [K15–K16]. HACo-Det reports weak average fine-grained metric-based performance and continuing generalisation problems [K15]; SenDetEX shows why short-sentence and surrounding-context evaluation require dedicated methods [K16]. Report only minimal coherent spans supported by the actual method, not arbitrary sentence flags or an inferred exact AI fraction.

### 3.7 Operating points, calibration, and passage selection

Accuracy and AUROC alone are insufficient. For any real computational method, report the operating threshold, FPR, TPR/recall at that FPR, FNR, sample sizes and uncertainty where available, plus positive predictive value (PPV) only under an explicitly justified prevalence model. PPV depends on prevalence: `PPV = TPR × prevalence / (TPR × prevalence + FPR × (1 − prevalence))`; therefore even a low FPR does not make a flag equivalent to a high posterior probability [K18, K24].

Calibrate on held-out data matched as closely as possible to deployment language, genre, length, generator, editing, translation, and relevant subgroups. Use document/author-level splits to avoid leakage. Do not transfer a document threshold to sentences or overlapping windows without validating that full segment-and-aggregate pipeline. Selecting only the maximum of many overlapping windows creates selection/multiplicity concerns; window-level FPR is not document-level FPR.

The evidence reviewed here establishes no universal token minimum, and this resource adopts none. A November 2026 preprint illustrates large length-dependent threshold changes and proposes length-conditional calibration and abstention [K25]; it motivates validation, not a fixed cutoff adopted by this resource. Abstain when length, domain, detector version, calibration, extraction, or subgroup evidence is outside the validated envelope. Multiple detectors are not independent votes unless their combined operating characteristics have been evaluated.

## 4. Confidence and Assessment Rules

### 4.1 Assessments

- **Detected — AI-origin externally established:** Tier A only, with confidence 3. Name the exact external evidence and matched span; identify the actual record reproducibly in the finding or verification trail, including access restrictions and limitations. A methodological `[K##]` citation alone is not external substantiation. Add “not a finding of intent or misconduct.”
- **Suspected:** An evidence-based review flag from Tier B–E, with confidence 1 or 2. Never abbreviate this as “detected.”
- **Inconclusive:** Conflicting evidence, inadequate span/quality, or an untestable hypothesis, with confidence 0 or 1 and an explanation.
- **No flag:** Not a classification of human authorship. Unflagged text remains untested or unsupported, not proven human-written.

Do not use confidence 0 for a positive AI-origin finding, confidence above 1 for Inconclusive, or Detected below confidence 3. The external evidence for Detected must be tied to the quoted span, not merely to the whole document or an earlier version; verify identity, timing, span correspondence, and chain of custody, and address copying, quotation, and subsequent editing.

### 4.2 Ordinal AI-origin confidence

| Score | Label | Meaning and permitted evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | **Not assessable** | No usable passage-level evidence, unusable extraction, or conflicts prevent a judgment. This is not zero probability and not a human-authorship finding. |
| **1** | **Low** | Weak, non-diagnostic support: text-internal observations, unmatched stylometry, an unverified report, or applicable evidence with major unresolved limitations. All purely stylistic findings stop here. |
| **2** | **Moderate** | Requires: (a) at least one actually executed Tier B/C method satisfying all tier requirements, with deployment-matched validation and documented residual error; and (b) a separately identified, passage-specific evidence family whose convergence and independence are affirmatively justified. A single Tier B/C output alone is insufficient. Repeated stylistic cues, language-model opinions, and outputs derived from the same features or score cannot establish independence. Multiple detectors require comparable evaluation of their combined passage-level operating characteristics. If either condition is not demonstrated, cap at 1. Record both evidence families and the independence rationale in the finding. It remains suspicion unless Tier A is present. |
| **3** | **High** | Strong, directly applicable Tier A external corroboration tied to the quoted span, with identity/chain and material alternatives checked. It does not quantify the probability of misconduct. |

Do not add scores, convert them to percentages, present them as calibrated probabilities, average them across passages, claim numerical intervals between labels, or use slash/fraction notation for ordinal confidence. Repetition of the same cue, multiple language-model opinions, or several detectors sharing the same features are not automatically independent convergence. Downgrade when short length, domain/language mismatch, editing, OCR, translation, quotation, templates, bias risk, extraction problems, or audit disagreement are outside the validated envelope or otherwise unresolved. A limitation explicitly covered by adequate setting-matched validation does not by itself force a further downgrade.

For the overall interpretation write, for example: “Evidence is limited; strongest passage confidence = 1 (low) on the 0–3 ordinal scale.” Do not emit an overall AI percentage.

## 5. Local Observation and Reporting Rules

1. **Preserve the source.** Never silently edit analysed text. Record file name/version if supplied, extraction method/quality, and whitespace normalisation.
2. **Define locations before judging.** Number paragraphs in source order and sentences within each paragraph using `P###.S##`; include native page numbers when present and a file identifier for multi-file input. Declare the treatment of headings, lists, tables, footnotes, front matter, and omitted content. For lines or offsets, declare indexing base and Unicode encoding. Every finding row must contain at least one locator that uniquely resolves to the quoted source span; do not emit a positive finding with an approximate or missing location. Never invent page numbers or quote from a lossy extraction as if checked against the original.
3. **Segment minimally and coherently.** A row should contain the smallest contiguous passage that supports the observation and alternatives; do not manufacture word-level boundaries. Keep quotations, references, code, templates, OCR-damaged regions, and formulaic required language separate when their production process differs.
4. **Observe before interpreting.** Describe only reproducible properties (for example, repeated sentence frames in specific lines). Explain why those properties might arise in human writing. Do not infer native language, race, disability, identity, intent, or honesty.
5. **Use human controls where available.** Matched samples must be comparable in writer, language, genre, purpose, time, and editing conditions. Lack of a baseline is a limitation, not permission to treat generic web prose as the author’s baseline.
6. **Compute only with real tools.** Record exact tool/model versions, command/settings, thresholds, validation set and its language/domain/length relation, FPR/FNR or other operating-point evidence where available, and date. Report missing information as missing.
7. **Challenge the flag.** Test translation, second-language writing without labelling the author, formulaic disciplinary style, templates, accessibility-related composition, editing, citation conventions, quotation, OCR, and ordinary style changes. Remove flags with no passage-specific support.
8. **Audit the strongest findings.** Where independent review is available, ask it to challenge quotes, locations, alternative explanations, and confidence. Reconcile or lower confidence; disclose when no audit was performed.
9. **Respect privacy.** Do not upload analysed text, writing samples, private or identifying metadata, analysed-document titles, or private record identifiers to third-party detectors, retrieval services, source-matching services, or search services without explicit user permission. Bibliographic freshness searches may use generic methodological terms and non-identifying, non-sensitive language, genre, or model labels. Omit any input-specific term that could disclose private information and record the resulting coverage limitation; if required evidence-specific coverage cannot be completed safely, verification fails.

## 6. Mandatory Freshness Protocol

The companion agent implements this protocol immediately before every text analysis. A passing result is bounded, not a guarantee of completeness.

### 6.1 Required gate

1. Read and validate this entire resource, including metadata, hierarchy, confidence table, and source ledger. Stop if any essential component is missing or contradictory.
2. Every gate must obtain and record both runtime UTC and at least one independent live HTTP `Date` header or trusted timestamp, with the source. If no independent value can be obtained or parsed, if the values resolve to materially different UTC dates after allowing for response timing, or if this resource's verification date is malformed or later than the validated current date, return **verification failed** and do not analyse the text. Never use this file’s verification date as today’s date.
3. Search from the last verification date **and, independently, the trailing 18 months**. Overlap prevents a falsely recent timestamp from narrowing the search. Include publication dates through the validated current date.
4. Use **at least two independent scholarly channels**, including at least one structured bibliographic index (OpenAlex, Crossref, Semantic Scholar, Scopus, or Web of Science) and primary publication metadata (ACL Anthology, PMLR, journal publisher, or arXiv for preprints). Before literature screening, record the intended method families and decisive KB source IDs. Use the primary-publication channel to verify retained candidates and every declared decisive record. General web results and snippets are discovery aids only.
5. Successfully execute every query family below on the structured-index channel and add input-specific terms for language, genre, and claimed generator only as permitted by Section 5, rule 9:
   - `("AI-generated text" OR "machine-generated text") AND (detection OR detector) AND (survey OR review OR benchmark)`
   - `("AI-generated text detection" OR "machine-generated text detection") AND (robustness OR "distribution shift" OR paraphrase OR adversarial OR bias OR calibration OR "false positive")`
   - `("human-AI coauthoring" OR "mixed authorship" OR "AI-assisted writing") AND (detection OR attribution OR sentence-level OR span-level OR passage-level)`
   - `("text watermarking" OR "content provenance") AND (LLM OR "AI-generated text") AND (robustness OR verification OR attack)`
6. Check decisive records at abstract and, where their content would change an operative rule, full-text level. Verify persistent identifiers and publication status. Search corrections/retractions and newer contradictory replications for the rules being used. Prefer peer-reviewed versions but record relevant preprints as such.
7. Compare all findings against Sections 1–5. A **material update** changes an operative conclusion, evidence tier, confidence cap, applicability restriction, or source integrity. A new method or benchmark is not automatically material, but cannot be cited as a basis for stronger confidence without verification and incorporation.
8. During an analysis run, **do not modify this methodological source of truth**. If material evidence exists, record the conflict and stop with **verification failed: knowledge-base update required**. Resume only after a separately authorised and independently reviewed update has amended the relevant rules and ledger, incremented the version, and updated the UTC verification date and review log; then rerun the complete gate. 9. Record UTC timestamp, actual date source, declared methods and decisive source IDs, exact services/endpoints, instantiated queries and parameters/filters, coverage windows, pagination or bounded result counts, decisive record IDs, additions/contradictions, access depth, failures, and outcome. A pass requires **all** of these completion checks: successful execution of every mandatory query family on the structured-index channel; predetermined and recorded result/page bounds with all bounded results screened; primary-publication verification of retained candidates and every declared decisive record; live persistent-identifier and current publication-status verification for every decisive record; correction/retraction checks; and the abstract or full-text access depth required by each operative rule. Disclose individual failed attempts and route them through documented scholarly alternatives. Any uncompleted item, privacy-related inability to perform a mandatory search, material unincorporated update, or failed date validation means **verification failed: no text analysis**.
10. Apply a passed gate only to the immediately following analysis request. Never reuse it for a later request, even for the same document. If later analysis uses a method or decisive source outside the declared set, rerun the complete gate.

## 7. Required Findings Representation

Use exactly this passage table in the agent’s required report:

| Passage ID | Verbatim passage text | Location in supplied text | Assessment | Observable evidence and alternatives | AI-origin confidence (0–3, not probability) | Governing KB source(s) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Retain a **Suspected** row only when, after challenge, it has passage-specific observable evidence, a governing source, a reasonable alternative explanation, and confidence of at least 1. Include an **Inconclusive** row only for material conflicting evidence or an important unassessable candidate; do not report every rejected hypothesis.

Each finding must contain:

- a verbatim contiguous span, with disclosed whitespace handling;
- reproducible page/section/paragraph/sentence/line/offset locations as available;
- `Detected — AI-origin externally established`, `Suspected`, or `Inconclusive`;
- observable evidence **and** the strongest reasonable human/alternative explanation in the evidence/alternatives cell;
- the numeric score, label, and concise justification in the confidence cell;
- applicable `[K##]` IDs; identify merely additive literature by full citation/URL in the verification trail, without inventing KB identifiers or using it to strengthen confidence;
- method-specific limitations in the evidence/alternatives cell. Quote each retained span in full and disclose whitespace handling; split long passages into consecutive sub-rows rather than abbreviating them.

If none meet the reporting threshold, return no finding rows and state: **“No passage-specific flags met the reporting threshold; this does not establish human authorship.”** Follow with a cautious overall interpretation, no aggregate percentage, the strongest passage confidence if any, methods and detector versions actually used, independent-audit status, and full verification trail.

(2025). “HACo-Det: A Study Towards Fine-Grained Machine-Generated Text Detection under Human-AI Coauthoring.” *ACL 2025*, 22015–22036. DOI: [10.18653/v1/2025.acl-long.1069](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.acl-long.1069). Peer-reviewed; full text. Reports weak metric-based word/sentence-level results, better fine-tuned performance in tested settings, and unresolved generalisation/context-window limits. Contraindicates exact human/AI ratios from this agent.
- **[K16] Sentence-level hybrid content.** Jiang, L., Wu, D., & Zheng, X. (2025). “SenDetEX: Sentence-Level AI-Generated Text Detection for Human-AI Hybrid Content via Style and Context Fusion.” *EMNLP 2025*, 5287–5302. DOI: [10.18653/v1/2025.emnlp-main.268](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.emnlp-main.268). Peer-reviewed; full text. Shows why document detectors do not trivially transfer to short sentences and why context matters. Its benchmark/model improvements are not externally calibrated probabilities here.
- **[K17] Stylistics-aware thresholds.** Wu, J., Wang, J., Liu, Z., et al. (2025). “MoSEs: Uncertainty-Aware AI-Generated Text Detection via Mixture of Stylistics Experts with Conditional Thresholds.” *EMNLP 2025*, 5786–5805. - **[K18] False-positive control and calibration.** Zhu, X., Ren, Y., Cao, Y., Lin, X., Fang, F., & Li, Y. - **[K19] Bounded counterevidence on expert human review.** Russell, J., Karpinska, M., & Iyyer, M. (2025). “People who frequently use ChatGPT for writing tasks are accurate and robust detectors of AI-generated text.” *ACL 2025*, 5342–5373. DOI: [10.18653/v1/2025.acl-long.267](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.acl-long.267). Peer-reviewed; full text. Majority vote of five frequent ChatGPT users performed near-perfectly on the study’s 300 articles; explanations often involved vocabulary and sentence structure. Its population, panel design, data, and article-level setting sharply limit transfer to this agent.
- **[K20] Multilingual short-text benchmark.** Macko, D., Kopál, J., Moro, R., & Srba, I. (2025). “MultiSocial: Multilingual Benchmark of Machine-Generated Text Detection of Social-Media Texts.” *ACL 2025*, 727–752. DOI: [10.18653/v1/2025.acl-long.36](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.acl-long.36). Peer-reviewed; publication metadata/abstract checked. Covers 22 languages, five platforms, 472,097 texts, and seven multilingual LLMs; finds platform-specific training effects. Supports explicit domain/language limits, not classification from informal language or errors.
- **[K21] Intersectional and inconsistent detector bias.** Stowe, K., Afanaseva, S., Raimundo, R. C., Sun, Y., & Patil, K. (2026). “Identifying Bias in Machine-generated Text Detection.” *ACL 2026*, 2383–2395. DOI: [10.18653/v1/2026.acl-long.109](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2026.acl-long.109). Peer-reviewed; publication metadata/abstract checked. Sixteen detectors showed inconsistent but consequential subgroup patterns, including elevated machine-generated labels for ELL writing and a non-White/White ELL disparity. Limits transfer and reinforces subgroup-aware caution without inferring user traits.
- **[K22] Professional-editing style confound.** Park, H., Jeong, G., & Kim, B. (2026). “Style as a Confound: False Positives in AI Detection of Non-Native Academic Writing.” *EMNLP 2026: Industry Track*, 1872–1887. DOI: [10.18653/2026.emnlp-industry.109](https://doi.org/10.18653/2026.emnlp-industry.109). Peer-reviewed; publication metadata/abstract checked. Across 135,389 original/edited manuscript pairs, 13 detectors responded inconsistently to the same authorship-preserving edits; reported human-text FPRs spanned 0–100% across conditions. The editing-service sample and pooled range are not a universal error rate.
- **[K23] Authentic student-work evaluation.** Sun, Y., Liao, Y., & Ma, X. (2026). “Trusting AI to detect AI? A systematic evaluation of the reliability and robustness of current AIGC detection tools for student academic work.” *Computers & Education*, 249, 105616. DOI: [10.1016/j.compedu.2026.105616](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2026.105616). Peer-reviewed; DOI metadata and indexed abstract checked. Reports over 280,000 authentic samples, 13 detectors, long-thesis versus short-coursework/code differences, disciplinary bias, and extensive evasion under its tested hybrid editing. These task-specific findings support abstention and caution, not a universal failure/success estimate.
- **[K24] Low-FPR evaluation on unseen settings.** Tufts, B., Zhao, X., & Li, L. (2025). “A Practical Examination of AI-Generated Text Detectors for Large Language Models.” *Findings of the ACL: NAACL 2025*, 4839–4856. DOI: [10.18653/v1/2025.findings-naacl.271](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.findings-naacl.271). Peer-reviewed; publication metadata/abstract checked. Seven detectors lost sensitivity on unseen data/models and moderate adversarial prompts; TPR at FPR 0.01 was as low as zero in a tested setting. This worst-case result is not a pooled estimate, but supports reporting TPR at an explicit FPR.
- **[K25] Length-conditional calibration—preprint.** Janga, R., & Zhan, J. (2026). “How Short Is Too Short? Length-Conditional Calibration and Abstention for Detecting AI-Generated Text.” [arXiv:2611.10901v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10901v1), submitted 7 November 2026. **Preprint, not peer-reviewed**; abstract checked. Tests length-conditioned thresholds, calibration, and abstention for three zero-shot detectors. Its numerical results are preliminary and establish no universal minimum length or transferable performance guarantee.
- **[K26] Contestable decision-support interface.** Li, J., Ye, M., Hu, X., Peng, C., Yin, X., & Wan, X. (2026). “When AI-Text Detectors Become Evidence: Rethinking Detection for Human Decisions.” *Findings of the ACL: EMNLP 2026*, 32727–32757. DOI: [10.18653/2026.findings-emnlp.1539](https://doi.org/10.18653/2026.findings-emnlp.1539). Peer-reviewed; publication metadata/abstract checked. In a controlled fixed-stimulus study, its complete AGENT-XTrust system/interface improved decisions and reduced overtrust relative to score-only and score-plus-explanation interfaces. It supports inspectable alternatives and contestability, not confidence in arbitrary generated rationales or transfer to other systems.
