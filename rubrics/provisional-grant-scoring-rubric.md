# Provisional Grant Proposal Scoring Rubric

Status: Draft for owner approval. Do not use for final scoring until explicitly approved.

## Scale

Score each criterion from 0 to 5:

- 0 — Not addressed or fundamentally unacceptable
- 1 — Major deficiencies; little credible support
- 2 — Significant weaknesses; partly credible
- 3 — Adequate; material weaknesses remain
- 4 — Strong; only minor weaknesses
- 5 — Outstanding; compelling and well supported

Weighted score = (criterion score / 5) × criterion weight. Total project score is the sum of weighted scores on a 0–100 scale.

## Criteria and Weights

| Criterion | Weight | Assessment focus |
|---|---:|---|
| Relevance and significance | 15 | Importance of the problem, fit to the funding objective, and likely contribution |
| Scientific quality and conceptual foundation | 20 | Clarity of research questions, theoretical grounding, engagement with prior scholarship, and validity of assumptions |
| Methodology and analysis plan | 20 | Design appropriateness, sampling, measures, controls, analytical methods, reproducibility, and bias mitigation |
| Feasibility and implementation | 15 | Work plan, milestones, resources, access, dependencies, and deliverability within the proposed period |
| Team capability and governance | 10 | Relevant expertise, role clarity, collaboration, leadership, and decision-making arrangements |
| Impact, dissemination, and sustainability | 10 | Plausible outcomes, beneficiaries, knowledge transfer, uptake, scalability, and durability |
| Ethics, risk, and responsible research | 5 | Ethical safeguards, data management, research integrity, inclusion, and risk controls |
| Value for money | 5 | Proportionality of costs to activities, outputs, risks, and expected benefits |
| **Total** | **100** | |

## Cross-Cutting Scoring Rules

1. Use only information in the proposal, reviewer evaluations, and verified peer-reviewed academic sources.
2. Apply the same interpretation, evidentiary threshold, scale, and weights to every project.
3. Do not infer missing claims in a proposal from external literature. External sources may test plausibility or resolve reviewer disagreement, but cannot repair an absent project commitment.
4. Separate proposal quality from reviewer quality. A reviewer’s unsupported assertion receives low evidentiary weight.
5. Record uncertainty. If evidence is genuinely inconclusive, use the score supported by the proposal itself and state the unresolved uncertainty rather than forcing false consensus.
6. Avoid double-counting one weakness across multiple criteria unless it independently affects each criterion; explain any repeated effect.
7. Round criterion scores to one decimal place and the total weighted score to one decimal place.
8. Rank ties by, in order: methodology and analysis plan; scientific quality and conceptual foundation; relevance and significance. If still tied, report a tie.

## Required Validation Fields

For each criterion record:

- Criterion and weight
- Proposal claim or evidence, with page/section reference
- Reviewer positions, attributed by reviewer identifier
- Agreement or contradiction status
- External peer-reviewed evidence used, with citation and persistent identifier or stable URL when available
- Rapporteur analysis
- Raw score (0–5)
- Weighted score
- Confidence: high, medium, or low
- Key limitation or condition

## Approval Rule

Before the first final assessment, present this rubric to the owner and ask for approval or amendments. Once approved for a batch, freeze the rubric and record its version in every project workbook and the master workbook. Never change criteria, weights, scoring anchors, or tie-breaks within a batch.
